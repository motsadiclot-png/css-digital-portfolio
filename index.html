My Academic Learning Reflection

Introduction

Starting my journey at Botswana Accountancy College (BAC) has been an important transition in my academic life. Moving from secondary school to tertiary education introduced me to a different learning environment, where independence, personal responsibility and dedication play a much bigger role. As a first-year Bachelor of Information and Data Analytics (BIDA) student, I have had to adjust to new academic expectations, unfamiliar learning tools and the responsibility of managing my own time. Although the transition has presented several challenges, it has also provided opportunities for personal growth and development.

Challenges Experienced

One of my biggest challenges has been managing my workload across four modules while maintaining a consistent study routine. Communication during our CSS group discussions was also difficult at times, making collaboration and coordination challenging. Additionally, completing the quizzes in Statistical Foundations for Data Analytics required consistent preparation and a good understanding of the concepts.

Another challenge was adapting to practical applications of technology. Using Microsoft Access and Excel for the first time in my Computer Technology practicals required me to familiarise myself with unfamiliar tools and develop new technical skills. I also encountered a personal academic challenge in Fundamental of Business Finance, where I came across accounting-related concepts despite having dropped Accounting in Grade 10.

Strategies Used to Overcome Challenges

To overcome these challenges, I have realised the importance of dedication, discipline and effective time management. I have been working on balancing my social life with my academic responsibilities by prioritising my studies and setting aside dedicated study time. I have also recognised the importance of minimising distractions, particularly putting away my phone when studying, so that I can concentrate better. These changes have helped me become more aware of how I use my time and approach my academic responsibilities.

Lessons and Personal Growth

One of the most important lessons I have learned is that, at university, nobody pushes you to succeed except yourself. Unlike secondary school, where teachers may provide more direct supervision, tertiary education requires students to take responsibility for their own progress. I have also developed a better understanding of financial responsibility through learning to budget my monthly P2,200 DTEF allowance. This has taught me to be more conscious of how I manage both my time and money.

Future Improvements

Going forward, I intend to improve how I manage my academic workload by planning my study sessions earlier and avoiding last-minute preparation. I also want to become more confident when interacting and communicating with others, particularly my group project members. Improving these areas will help me become a more organised, independent and effective student.

Overall, my first-year experience has taught me that academic success requires more than understanding what is taught in class. It also requires discipline, adaptability, responsibility and a willingness to continuously improve.
